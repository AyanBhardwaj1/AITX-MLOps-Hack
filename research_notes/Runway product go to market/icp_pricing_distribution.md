# Runway GTM: ICP, Willingness to Pay, Pricing Model, Distribution Channels

Research date: 2026-10-31. US startup focus. Method: web search only. **rho.co could not be fetched directly** (the host is outside this session's allowed network domains), so Rho facts come from search snippets of rho.co pages and third parties. Most figures below come from secondary aggregators summarizing primary reports. Where a primary report could not be opened, this is flagged. Hard data appears under "Cited Findings". Opinion and inference appear under "Inferences".

**Naming flag (important):** "Runway" is already the brand of an existing FP&A product (Runway Financial, runway.com). Listings put it at roughly $350/mo (Standard) and $800/mo (Growth), or at custom, demo-only pricing ([Causal/Runway pricing roundup](https://raisereadybook.pages.dev/blog/best-financial-model-software-startup-founders-2026); low confidence). This collides on SEO, trademark and positioning, and the founder should weigh it before investing in brand and content.

## 1. ICP: who feels runway and SaaS-waste pain most and has budget?

### Takeaway
The best first buyer is the **seed to Series A founder or first finance hire at a 5–50 person, VC-backed US startup that already banks on Rho (later Mercury)**. Runway anxiety is acute for them, since seed-to-A gaps now run about 20–26 months, and they have no CFO. The best *second* motion is **fractional CFOs and startup accounting firms** (one buyer, many clients). Accelerators and VCs are a distribution partner and portfolio-monitoring upsell, not the first paying ICP. Pre-seed founders feel the pain but have the least SaaS spend to save and the least budget.

### Cited Findings
**Runway pressure (why the pain is acute now)**
- Median time from seed to Series A is reported at about 2.2 years, up from 1.5 years in 2019. Another Carta-based figure is 616 days (~20 months) in Q2 2025, and the median gap across all stages was 696 days (~23 months) in Q2 2025. These are secondary summaries of Carta data; the primary report was not opened — [SaaStr on 3,365 startups](https://www.saastr.com/your-seed-round-now-needs-to-last-3-years-what-3365-startups-tell-us-about-the-new-series-a-timeline); [Carta State of Private Markets Q3 2025](https://carta.com/data/state-of-private-markets-q3-2025/); [Carta 2025 in review](https://www.carta.com/data/state-of-private-markets-q4-2025)
- Carta companies raised 401 new seed rounds in Q1 2025 (−28% YoY) totaling $1.2B (−37%). Seed rounds were ~40% of new venture rounds but only 9.4% of cash in Q3 2025. These are Carta-platform counts, not a national total — [Crowdfund Insider on Carta](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2025/12/256059-early-stage-valuations-keep-rising-down-rounds-growing-less-frequent-carta-private-markets-insights/); [VC Cafe State of Seed 2025](https://www.vccafe.com/2025/12/12/the-state-of-seed-and-pre-seed-in-2025-bigger-bets-leaner-teams-and-the-ai-distortion-field/)
- Median 2025 seed: ~$4.0M cash raised and $20M post-money (another source says ~$3.6M; uncertain) — [speedrun substack on Carta State of Seed](https://speedrun.substack.com/p/8-takeaways-from-cartas-state-of)
- The SaaStr piece argues that "your seed round needs to last 3+ years, not 18 months" (opinion, single source) — [SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/your-seed-round-now-needs-to-last-3-years-what-3365-startups-tell-us-about-the-new-series-a-timeline)

**SaaS spend and waste (size of the "waste" prize)**
- Zylo 2026 SaaS Management Index: median SaaS spend of **$9,455 per employee per year**, with a reported average of $11,530. About **36% of licenses go unused**. Average wasted spend on unused licenses is $19.8M per organization, ranging from **$3.8M for 1–500 employee orgs** to $80.6M at 10,001+. Zylo's sample is enterprise/mid-market heavy, and the primary report was not opened — [Zylo: how much is wasted](https://zylo.com/blog/how-much-wasted-on-saas-spend); [Zylo SaaS stats](https://zylo.com/blog/saas-stats-it-strategy); [letsdatascience summary](https://letsdatascience.com/news/zylo-reports-saas-spend-rising-with-ai-dea3e5cc)
- Zylo: the average company manages ~305 SaaS apps (secondary) — [spotsaas](https://www.spotsaas.com/blog/saas-statistics)
- Vertice: SaaS spend per employee is "flatlining at $9,200". An aggregator cites $9,324 in Q2 2026. The trend runs ~$7,900 (2023) → $8,700 (2024) → ~$9,100 (end-2025) — [Vertice: SaaS spend per employee](https://www.vertice.one/insights/saas-spend-per-employee); [Vertice blog](https://www.vertice.one/blog/saas-spend-per-employee)
- Vertice SaaS inflation was reported at 12–16% YoY in 2026. The figures conflict across aggregators (12.2%, 13.2%, 16.4%), so treat the exact number as unresolved — [Vertice inflation index](https://www.vertice.one/i/inflation-index-cfos)
- Cledara (SMB/startup-focused) puts the waste estimate at **25–35% of SaaS budgets** on unused or underutilized licenses (March 2026 post). Its other posts say 30–40% of subscriptions are substantially underutilized and cite 30–50% of licenses unused (via G2) — [Cledara: why SaaS management](https://cledara.com/blog/why-companies-need-saas-management); [Cledara G2 summer 2025](https://cledara.com/blog/trends-from-g2s-summer-2025-saas-spend-management-grid)
- A Cledara 2021 survey of 251 UK firms (20–500 employees) found only 14% think all their SaaS subscriptions add value. This is old and UK-only — [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/2021/10/23/report-14-of-decision-makers-feel-all-saas-subscriptions-benefit-their-business)
- Cledara has a startup-specific report ("How Do Startups Actually Spend on SaaS After They Get Funded?") but its numbers were not retrieved — [Cledara](https://go.cledara.com/managing-spend-after-funding)

**Neobank startup customer base (TAM proxies)**
- Mercury: ~**300,000 customers**, with a 2.5x YoY rise in applications in Q1 2026 — [American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/mercury-raises-200-million-after-occ-bank-charter-approval). It reported ~$650M annualized revenue at end of Q3 2025 (up from $500M at end of 2024) and 40% customer growth through 2025 — [Fortune, Nov 2025](https://www.fortune.com/2025/11/07/exclusive-mercury-fintech-valuation-650-million-2025-annualized-revenue-immad-akhund-interview)
- Rho: no public customer count found. A Clerky partnership post says "thousands of startups," naming Perplexity, Cal.com and Public.com. Rho says its customers include more than a dozen public companies and that it serves startups "from incorporation through IPO". Its banking partner is Webster Bank, N.A. (previously Evolve) — [Clerky](https://www.clerky.com/blog/rho-partnership); [Rho startups](https://www.rho.co/solutions/startups); [Wikipedia: Rho Technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rho_Technologies)
- Brex/Ramp: startup customer counts were not researched within the budget (gap).

### Inferences
- **Sizing the waste per customer (assumption-driven, not sourced):** Zylo/Vertice per-employee figures (~$9.2–9.5K/yr) skew to larger companies. Say a 15-person seed startup spends a more conservative $3–6K per employee per year (assumption), or $45–90K/yr. At Cledara's 25–35% waste, that is roughly **$11–31K/yr (~$950–2,600/mo) of addressable waste**. Even at half that, a $50–150/mo tool pays back 5–20x on SaaS savings alone. The founder should validate this with real Rho feeds from 10–20 design partners.
- **Segment ranking (opinion):**
  1. **Seed–Series A (5–50 FTE), no full-time CFO.** This group has the highest pain-to-budget ratio. They have real burn ($100K–$500K/mo), runway questions from the board, and enough SaaS sprawl to make waste findings meaningful.
  2. **Fractional CFOs and startup accounting firms.** They monitor 10–40 clients and need autopilot-style monthly reviews, so they are a channel and buyer in one. Rho is actively building this channel (see §3).
  3. **Series A–B finance leads.** They have budget but likely already use Ramp/Brex spend tools, Pilot, Runway Financial or Mosaic, so they are a harder sale.
  4. **Pre-seed.** Pain is high but SaaS spend is tiny. Treat them as a free-tier and SEO audience that converts later.
  5. **Accelerators/VCs (portfolio monitoring).** Distribution partners and a later enterprise-ish tier. Visible and Carta own the investor-reporting workflow, so this is not the first revenue.
- Mercury's 300K customers vs Rho's "thousands" means Rho-first gives a small TAM. Rho-first is a wedge (partner access, less noise). Mercury support is likely necessary to reach scale.

### Gaps
- No public Rho customer count or segment mix. rho.co was unreachable from this session.
- No startup-specific (sub-50 FTE) per-employee SaaS spend figure from Zylo or Vertice. Cledara's startup report numbers were not retrieved.
- No hard data on what share of US VC-backed startups bank with Rho vs Mercury vs Brex. The claim that "50%+ of YC companies use Mercury (early 2023)" comes only from a low-reliability AI-style summary — [onepage-research](https://onepage-research.sliplane.app/products/mercury).

## 2. Willingness to pay and pricing models

### Takeaway
Comparable startup-finance tools cluster at **$25–$250/mo flat for founders** and **$99–$600+/mo for done-for-you services**. Pure %-of-savings is used only by negotiation services serving larger spenders (and even Vertice and Vendr are mostly subscriptions with savings guarantees). For a tool that "finds $X/mo in waste," the best fit is a **free runway tier, a flat founder plan of ~$49–$149/mo with a "pays for itself or it's free" guarantee, and a per-client firm plan**. Percentage-of-savings is a poor fit at seed-stage dollar amounts.

### Cited Findings
**Benchmark prices (startup finance tools, $/mo)**
- **Pry** (startup FP&A, acquired by Brex in 2024): Solo plan ~$50/mo ($45 annual). Paid tiers are listed at $150–$400/mo, the top tier is ~$540/mo, and a limited free plan exists. Aggregator data, moderately consistent — [toolradar Pry pricing](https://toolradar.com/tools/pry/pricing); [G2 Pry pricing](https://www.g2.com/products/pry/pricing); [Capterra CA](https://www.capterra.ca/software/1014931/pry-financials)
- **Causal**: its pricing page lists a $99 reporting tier and a $250 modelling tier, plus a startup discount ($99/mo for 6 months for teams under 10 that have raised under $500K). A later blog says Causal was acquired by Lucanet (Dec 2023) and went enterprise-only. The sources conflict — [Causal pricing](https://www.causal.app/pricing); [Capterra Causal](https://www.capterra.com/p/217236/Causal/pricing/); [raisereadybook blog](https://raisereadybook.pages.dev/blog/best-financial-model-software-startup-founders-2026)
- **Runway Financial** (FP&A): ~$350/mo Standard and $800/mo Growth per one listing, or custom pricing per another. Described as the "wrong tool for pre-seed and seed founders" (opinion) — [raisereadybook blog](https://raisereadybook.pages.dev/blog/best-financial-model-software-startup-founders-2026)
- **Puzzle** (AI accounting for startups): Starter ~$25/mo annual or $30/mo monthly. Its free plan (pre-revenue, ≤50 transactions/mo) was reportedly turned into a 2-month promo in July 2026, and the $0 Starter was removed in Sept 2026. Full-service accounting launched at ~$171/mo (≤$10K monthly expenses) — [usagepricing Puzzle activity](https://www.usagepricing.com/blueprint/activity/puzzle-2026-09-20-full-service-accounting-launch); [layer3labs Puzzle guide](https://www.layer3labs.io/guides/puzzle-accounting-pricing-guide); [spotsaas Puzzle](https://www.spotsaas.com/product/puzzle/pricing)
- **Pilot** (bookkeeping service): no free plan. Essentials is listed at $99/mo (PricingSaaS, CostBench) or $199/mo (Capterra), and Core at $299–$599/mo — [PricingSaaS Pilot](https://pricingsaas.com/companies/pilot); [Capterra Pilot pricing](https://www.capterra.com/p/186313/Pilot/pricing/); [Pilot blog](https://pilot.com/blog/puzzle-alternatives)
- Rho itself has "no subscription tiers from day zero" (free banking), so Runway layers on top of a free bank — [Rho/Stripe Atlas partner page](https://www.rho.co/partner/stripe-atlas)
- Visible and Finta: no pricing found in this pass (gap).

**Savings-based models (Vendr/Vertice)**
- **Vertice** charges a flat fee based on annual SaaS and cloud spend, with a savings guarantee: it refunds the shortfall if savings don't exceed the subscription. It claims ~20% typical savings — [Vertice savings analysis](https://www.vertice.one/savings-analysis); [Vertice negotiation](https://www.vertice.one/platform/saas-purchasing/saas-contract-negotiation); [zoftwarehub](https://zoftwarehub.com/products/vertice/overview?click=tab)
- **Vendr**: the entry "Platform Access" tier is ~$12,000/yr (subscription). It claims 17.13% average savings and 9.5x ROI (vendor marketing) — [Vendr buy](https://www.vendr.com/buy); [Vendr vs Vertice G2](https://www.g2.com/compare/vendr-vendr-vs-vertice)
- A niche competitor (NoSaveNoPay) charges **25% of verified contract savings** against a documented baseline. This is a self-interested claim — [NoSaveNoPay vs Vendr](https://nosavenopay.pages.dev/compare-vendr)

### Inferences
- **Recommended pricing pattern (opinion):**
  - **Free:** connect Rho, see burn, runway and the forecast range, plus 1 waste finding preview. This is the acquisition hook and pairs with the SEO calculator.
  - **Founder/Pro ~$79/mo (range $49–$149):** full waste findings, Copilot and Autopilot monthly memo. Add a guarantee: "If Runway doesn't find at least 3x its price in savings in 90 days, it's free" (borrowed from Vertice's guarantee mechanic, adapted to SMB). This sits between Puzzle/Pry ($25–50) and Causal/Pilot ($99–250+), and is defensible if the median finding is ≥$500/mo.
  - **Firm/Portfolio:** ~$20–40 per client per month, with a minimum (e.g., $199/mo) for fractional CFOs, accounting firms and accelerators. Multi-client dashboards plus white-label Autopilot memos.
  - **Avoid %-of-savings as the primary model.** Waste at seed scale is ~$1–3K/mo (inference in §1), so a 20–25% fee yields $200–750/mo with heavy attribution disputes ("I would have cancelled that anyway"). It also creates a one-time revenue cliff after cleanup. Use savings as the *value metric in messaging and the guarantee*, not as the billing unit.
  - **Avoid per-seat pricing:** buyers are 1–2 people (founder plus bookkeeper or CFO), so seats don't scale with value.
- The "SaaS waste" finding is front-loaded: most savings come in month 1. Retention therefore must rest on recurring value (runway forecast, monthly Autopilot review, renewal alerts, new-tool detection). This argues for anchoring price on the *monthly review*, not on savings.

### Gaps
- No direct survey data on founder WTP for runway/burn tools. Validate with Van Westendorp or price tests among design partners.
- Visible.vc, Finta, Fondo and Mosaic current pricing not retrieved.
- No public %-of-savings benchmarks for SMB-scale SaaS management (Cledara, Spendflo, Tropic pricing not retrieved).

## 3. Distribution channels: what realistically reaches them

### Takeaway
The highest-leverage channels, in order:
1. **Rho partnership** (perks listing, accounting-firm channel, API partner). Rho is actively building a partner, perks and accounting-referral org in 2026.
2. **Fractional CFO and startup accountant channel** (one sale reaches 10–40 startups).
3. **Accelerator perks pages** (YC/Techstars/Antler and Rho's own accelerator partners like StartX).
4. **A free runway/burn calculator plus an open-core repo for SEO and developer trust.**

Product Hunt and HN are one-time spikes. Note that the "Runway" name will hurt SEO against runway.com.

### Cited Findings
**Rho-specific (partner/API/perks)**
- Per search snippets of rho.co, Rho runs: a general **Partner Program** (automated spend management partnerships); an **accounting/CAS firm partner program with multi-client tooling** (rho.co/accountants); **Rho Perks** (vendor discounts, claimed to total "over $1M"); and a referral program — [Rho partners](https://rho.co/partners); [Accessing Partner Perks in your Rho account](https://www.rho.co/hc/accessing-partner-perks-in-your-rho-account); [Kruze Consulting on Rho](https://kruzeconsulting.com/partners/rho/)

The latter was posted around Sept 2026, and its posting indicates the program is still being built — [M13 jobs: Director Perks & Rewards](https://jobs.m13.co/companies/rho-2/jobs/62201496-director-perks-rewards); [M13 jobs: Law Firm & Accounting Partnerships Lead](https://jobs.m13.co/companies/rho-2/jobs/92744727-law-firm-accounting-partnerships-lead)

The Stripe channel gets $1,100 on a $20K deposit plus $500 on $10K card spend (aggregator, checked Oct 1 2026) — [Clerky](https://www.clerky.com/blog/rho-partnership); [superscout Rho](https://superscout.co/perk/rho)

- Rho's listed integrations include QuickBooks Online, NetSuite, Sage Intacct, Dynamics 365, Xero and **Puzzle**. A startup-finance tool (Puzzle) is already an integration partner, which shows a precedent — [Wikipedia/Rho integrations summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rho_Technologies); [rho.co llms.txt (search snippet)](https://www.rho.co/llms.txt)

**Other banks' perks and marketplaces**
- Mercury runs a perks marketplace (via Builtfirst), reported at 50+ software partners including AWS, Azure, Google Cloud, QuickBooks and 1Password. Aggregator data is inconsistent. Mercury integrations are listed on Zapier and Make, and no formal Mercury API partner program was found — [Mercury perks/Builtfirst](https://mercury.com/perks/builtfirst); [Mercury support: using perks](https://support.mercury.com/hc/en-us/articles/28771877727892-Using-perks); [perkstack](https://perkstack.co/catalog/mercury-perks-marketplace); [Zapier Mercury](https://zapier.com/apps/bank-mercury/integrations)

**Case studies**
- **Mercury:** the CEO is a repeat YC founder and former YC part-time partner. Mercury reportedly had 1,500 day-one signups at its April 2019 launch, ~40% MoM early growth, and ~60% of acquisition from word of mouth and referrals. It became the default bank for many YC batches (low-reliability secondary summary; verify) — [onepage-research Mercury](https://onepage-research.sliplane.app/products/mercury); [stackmatix](https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/mercury-for-startups)
- **Plausible (open-source + hosted):** fully bootstrapped, reached $1M ARR and later ~$3.1M ARR by late 2024 (newsletter figure). The self-hosted Community Edition releases every ~6 months and withholds premium features (funnels, ecommerce goals) "to ensure a protective barrier around our cloud offering". - **Pry → Brex:** Pry (startup FP&A) was acquired by Brex in 2024, which shows that banks and card companies buy startup-finance tools as wedge features. This is from background knowledge and was not re-verified in this session.
- **Puzzle:** integrated into Rho's integrations list (above), showing that a bank-integration path exists for a startup-finance tool.

### Inferences
- **Path to becoming a Rho-recommended app (opinion, ordered steps):**
  1. Use the hackathon relationship to get a Rho partner contact and production API key provisioning (keys are provisioned through Rho).
  2. Apply to the **Rho Perks** listing with a founder offer (e.g., 3 months free of Pro). The newly hired Perks & Rewards director needs inventory.
  3. Pitch the **accounting/fractional-CFO partnerships lead** on Runway as the multi-client "monthly review" tool that makes Rho stickier for firms. This aligns with Rho's stated 2026 hiring priorities.
  4. Pursue a listing in Rho's integrations directory alongside Puzzle.
  5. Propose a co-marketing "Rho runway report", using anonymized aggregate burn and SaaS-waste data, as content Rho can distribute.

  Risk: Rho could build this natively (cf. Brex acquiring Pry). Mitigate by going multi-bank (Mercury, Brex, Plaid) by month 6–12.
- **Channel priority with rough expectations (opinion):**
  1. Rho perks plus partner co-marketing: low CAC, small but high-intent pool.
  2. Fractional CFO and accountant channel. Each firm brings 10–40 clients. Offer a revenue share (e.g., 20%) or a free firm dashboard. This mirrors the QuickBooks ProAdvisor/Gusto accountant model (that playbook was not researched in depth here).
  3. Accelerator perks: StartX, Techstars, Antler, YC Deals. Free for 6–12 months for batch companies.
  4. Free "runway calculator" and "SaaS waste checker" tools, plus an open-core GitHub repo with a self-hosted runway engine. Keep Autopilot, Copilot and multi-bank as hosted-only (Plausible-style barrier).
  5. Product Hunt/HN launch for the initial spike and credibility.
  6. Founder content: monthly anonymized "startup SaaS waste index" from aggregated data.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm Rho's partner application process, revenue-share terms, or whether Rho's developer portal has a public third-party app marketplace (rho.co blocked in this session). **Action: allow rho.co in network settings, or contact Rho's partnerships team directly.**
- Brex/Ramp integration marketplace terms, YC Deals/Techstars perks submission processes, and Visible/Finta growth case studies were not researched within the tool budget.
- No hard data on conversion rates from bank perks listings.

## 4. Early metrics benchmarks (activation, free-to-paid, CAC/payback)

### Takeaway
Plan around **~36% activation**, **3–5% free-to-paid conversion for freemium (~8–10% for time-boxed trials or PLG with good onboarding)**, and **SMB CAC payback of 8–12 months (ACV under $15K)**. A partner-led channel should beat payback because CAC is near zero.

### Cited Findings
- Average SaaS activation rate is about 36% (Lenny Rachitsky and Yuriy Timen research, via secondary source) — [Userpilot](https://userpilot.com/blog/freemium-to-premium)
- Freemium-to-paid: typical B2B SaaS is 2–5%. First Page Sage reports a 3.7% average for traditional freemium, with a range of 1–10% across sources — [Monetizely](https://www.getmonetizely.com/articles/freemium-conversion-rate-the-key-metric-that-drives-saas-growth-3588c); [Crazy Egg](https://www.crazyegg.com/blog/free-to-paid-conversion-rate/?facet2=pdf)
- ProductLed survey (600+ B2B SaaS): ~9% of free accounts convert when companies first move to PLG, and PQL-targeted users convert at roughly 3x that — [Userpilot freemium SaaS](https://userpilot.com/blog/freemium-saas/)
- Kyle Poyar analysis (Jan 2026, 200 B2B products): median free-trial conversion ~8%, with B2B at 6–10%. Secondary, not verified against the original — [Flint](https://www.flint.com/blog/b2b-saas-free-trial-conversion-rate-statistics)
- SaaStr opinion: freemium "still probably need[s] 50 million active users" to work as a standalone business model — [SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/freemium-is-back-the-ai-edition-but-youll-still-probably-need-50-million-active-users-for-freemium-to-actually-work-as-a-business-model/)
- CAC payback benchmarks:
  - Optifai benchmark (939 B2B SaaS, Q2 2025–Q1 2026): SMB (ACV under $15K) 8–12 months; overall median 15 months; mid-market 14–18 months.
  - Benchmarkit: B2B median 18 months in 2024 (14 the prior year); SMB with ACV under $5K averaged ~9 months.
  - Aleph × Benchmarkit 2026: median 16 months.
  - KeyBanc 2025: ~20 months median.

  All are secondary summaries — [Rework CAC payback](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/growth-frameworks/cac-payback-optimization); [Aleph](https://www.getaleph.com/answers/cac-payback-period-saas-2026); [SaaS Hero](https://saashero.net/customer-retention/cac-payback-period-benchmarks-saas/)

### Inferences
- **Targets for Runway's first 6 months (opinion):**
  - Connect-bank activation of ≥40%, where "activated" means seeing a runway number plus ≥1 waste finding. The Rho OAuth/API key step is the main friction point.
  - Free-to-paid of 5–8% (better than the freemium average, because a dollar-denominated waste finding creates an obvious ROI moment).
  - Blended CAC under ~$300 at a ~$79/mo price, giving payback of ≤4 months, well under the SMB 8–12 month benchmark. Achievable only if most signups come via partner, perk and accountant channels rather than paid ads.
- **Unit-economics sanity check (illustrative):** 100 paying founders at $79 = ~$95K ARR. Reaching $1M ARR needs ~1,050 founder accounts, or e.g. 40 firms × 25 clients × $30 (≈$360K ARR) plus ~650 direct accounts. The firm channel is the faster path to meaningful ARR.
- Logo churn risk is high for seed startups (they die, get acquired, or graduate to a CFO). Plan for 3–5% monthly logo churn (assumption; no source found) and lean on firm and portfolio accounts for retention.

### Gaps
- No fintech-specific (startup-finance tools) activation or conversion benchmarks found. All figures are general B2B SaaS.
- No primary OpenView/ICONIQ/High Alpha 2025–2026 report was opened. An OpenView "3.2% median freemium" attribution seen on a low-quality site could not be verified and was excluded.
- No churn benchmarks for seed-stage-customer SaaS found.
